## Findings ledger (evidence, not vibes)

- **RFA-0.8 rung 7 (2026-08-26): the fleet, half one whole and half two partial, and five things building it settled.**
  - **Wire 10.3 item 8's digest rule cannot fire, and that is a property of the grammar rather than a bug in either.** Item 8 says a NON-LOCAL claimant blocked by a `local/…` key gets an opaque HMAC digest instead of the key. Item 2 says a non-local claimant's keys may only begin `room/<handle>` or its own `<home>`. Item 5 says intersection is prefix-or-equal on SEGMENTS, so two keys whose first segments differ never intersect. Put together, no validated guest key can ever be blocked by a `local/…` key, and the digest branch is unreachable through `claim`. Proved by exhaustion over every shape a guest could plausibly send, and pinned as a property test that fails if the grammar is ever widened. The rule is RETAINED (it costs nothing, the spec states it, and any future shared namespace would need it immediately) and both specs now carry the note, because an implementer who does not know this either writes a test that cannot pass or "fixes" the grammar until it can.
  - **`claimTokens` was module-global, so two hubs in one process shared one fence table.** Found by writing the restart test the rung asked for: an in-process "restart" presented a token minted by the PREVIOUS `RoomHub` and was believed, so the test passed for the wrong reason and would have certified the opposite of the guarantee. It is a per-instance field now. In production this never bit, because one hub owns one process; it bit the moment something tried to prove the guarantee. The two halves of the fence have opposite persistence requirements and now have two different storage decisions to match: the GRANT is a task field, persisted, because its job is refusing future claims (item 7 calls a process-local grant map non-conformant); the TOKEN is in memory and dies with the process, because a fence that survives is a fence somebody else can find.
  - **Widening only means anything if a claim by the current owner is not a re-claim.** Item 6 requires that widening "never damages the grant already held", but `claim` requires `state === "submitted"` and `owner === null`, so a holder's second claim would die at that check before item 6 could apply. The only coherent reading is that a `claim` BY THE CURRENT OWNER of a non-terminal task is a widening: it adds keys, and it deliberately does not touch `attempt` or mint a new `claim_token`, because both would invalidate the fence the holder is still using, which is the literal damage the item forbids. Written down as decision D1 in the design note rather than left as an implementation habit.
  - **The reservation offer lives on the task, so the approver approves what was offered.** Item 6 has three parties (the hub offers after three refused widenings, a creator/host/human approves over `update`, and the grant is taken on the creator's authority). If `update` had accepted arbitrary keys, the approver would be granting something nobody offered and the audit trail would not show what was agreed. The third refusal writes `reservation_offer` onto the task and carries it in that refusal's `data`; `approve_reservation` grants exactly those keys, and re-checks them against live grants first because the offer may be minutes old.
  - **A hardcoded `"0.1.8"` in a CLI test had to be edited by hand to bump the wire version**, which is the same shape as the stated counts this repository has watched rot twice. It asserts against `RFA_SPEC_VERSION` now: what the test is for is that `rfa version` reports the hub's protocol version, not which version that happens to be today.
  - **The version bump is gated, not editorial.** Spec 16.1 permits advertising `0.1.9` only with 10.3's `resources[]` validation and intersection refusal plus section 8's `would_deadlock`. Both are true, so `RFA_SPEC_VERSION`, INTEROP.md's header and its claim-lease section moved in the same change; INTEROP's own scar is a document that said one version while the hub said another.
  - **The 0.1.8-era interop client still works, and that is the compatibility promise proved with the artifact rather than a private harness.** `interop/rfa_min.py`, untouched and predating `resources[]` entirely, was run against a throwaway 0.1.9 hub: it joined, read the roster, declared presence, created and CLAIMED a task with no `resources[]`, completed it with evidence, ran a listen cycle and left with its token revoked. The hub reported `Wire 0.1.9` and `spec_version: "0.1.9"` in the same session. That is item 1 of wire 10.3 demonstrated end to end: a claim without `resources[]` behaves exactly as it did in 0.1.8.
  - **The watch's counter key resets for a local agent that REJOINS, and that is inherent rather than a bug.** RFA-0.6 sect. 5.6 fixes the key as `peer_id ?? principal ?? member.id` and says "a local member keeps the existing per-member key". A local agent carries no principal, so a fresh join mints a fresh member id and a fresh counter; a peer with a `peer_id`, which is what the watch defends against, keeps its key across flaps. Found while writing the test, which first simulated flaps by rejoining as a new member each time and saw the watch never fire. The test now flaps ONE membership, which is the shape the watch is keyed to see, and this residue is written here rather than left for someone to rediscover as a false negative.
  - **What half two ships and what it cannot prove.** `max_claims_per_member` (3) and `task_actions_per_min` (20) were both MEASURED ABSENT in Appendix F (one member held four concurrent claims and ran 22 unthrottled mutating calls) and now enforce, keyed `peer_id ?? principal ?? member.id` per RFA-0.6 sect. 5.6, with the windows moved off the member record and onto the room so leave-and-rejoin cannot reset a peer's budget. The greedy-peer watch fires at THREE offline-release flaps from one identity inside TEN MINUTES, counted per flap and not per released task, because a member holding three tasks and dropping them all is one event and counting the tasks would page an operator for a single network drop; it counts STATES and not a rate, which is the whole point, since a rate has no denominator at zero traffic and the room this defends is quiet by construction. Auto-hold exists and is OFF by default, because a hold is an intervention. The cross-org memory quarantine stamps `requester_home` and room on every `/memories` write and withholds a guest-attributed block from OTHER turns' compiled prompt until the consolidation lane promotes it, readable by `view` throughout. **None of the guest-facing branches has been exercised live**: every member on this instance is `home: "local"`, no admission record exists, and `peer_id` is absent throughout, so they are unit-tested through the same force-the-home internals idiom `approvalext.test.ts` already uses, and the cold-start interop path could not drive a real guest for the same reason. Stated here rather than implied to work.

- **RFA-0.8 rung 6 (2026-08-26): NOT BUILT, and the two checks that stopped it are worth more than the rung would have been.** The rung is spec'd as the last-resort tier gated on churn, so it was gated on two checks before any edit. Both failed.
  - **Check one, the trigger: no pack needs it.** The instance runs exactly two packs. `pm-agent` is `Read, Grep, Glob`. `linear-scribe` adds `mcp__rfa__ask` and two Linear tools, and its one acting tool writes to a remote SaaS workspace, not to the pack tree. NEITHER declares a guarded built-in, so neither is even a rung-5 writing pack; the only writing pack that has ever existed here is `filer`, the throwaway built for rung 5's fence proof, which writes to its own scratch by design. `probe-shape` and `test-agent` are residue directories with no `agent.md`. One hub directory exists on this machine and no pack in it declares `Write`, `Edit` or `NotebookEdit`. Rung 5's scratch surfaces already serve every writing pack that exists, which is precisely the condition under which building rung 6 would be speculative.
  - **Check two, the buried assumption: the pack tree is not under version control, and nothing noticed because nothing reads it.** Sect. 8.2's lifecycle is entirely git (commit on a run branch, merge into the shared tree, MERGE_HEAD as the in-progress marker, `git merge --abort` as the rollback, a branch recorded on the task). `rfa init` writes a `.gitignore` and NEVER runs `git init`; there is no `.git` anywhere under `~/rfa/acme` at any depth; `git rev-parse` in the hub directory says "not a git repository". The subtree map's first row calls the pack definition, prompt, `skills/` and `evals/` "the git subset for merge-back history" and that subset is tracked by no repository in existence.
  - **The `.gitignore` is what makes the assumption invisible, and reading it carefully is what settles the design fork.** Its header says "rfa: runtime state, secrets and logs (owned by the tool)" and it excludes `.rfa/`, `agents/*/state/` and the knowledge clones. It is written for an operator who versions their hub directory THEMSELVES; the tool does not. So rung 6 cannot just add `git init` to `rfa init`, because it has to answer who owns the repository: (a) RFA initializes one in the hub directory, which collides with or nests inside an operator who already versions that folder; (b) RFA uses whatever repository already covers the path, which puts a pack's run branches and merge commits into the operator's own history and cannot assume the pack tree sits at the repo root; or (c) RFA keeps a private repository it owns (a `.rfa/`-side git dir with the pack tree as its work tree), which keeps the merge machinery out of the operator's history and is NOT what sect. 8.2 describes. That fork is a design question with an operator-facing answer, not an implementation detail, and it is the reason "days" is the wrong estimate.
  - **Found while checking: `sandbox.isolation` is inert.** The schema accepts `none | worktree | container` (`src/agentdef.ts`) and NO production module reads the field; the only other mentions are a scaffold comment and two tests. So the `worktree` value rung 6 is supposed to retire configures nothing today, which lowers the migration cost when the rung is built and is a live documentation trap until then: an operator can set `isolation: container` and get exactly `none`. Not changed here, because deciding that migration is a rung-6 build item and the rung is not being built.
  - **What the research assumed.** W5 sect. 2.3 argues the CoW clone over a worktree because "the hub template gitignores `.rfa/`, `agents/*/state/` and knowledge clones, so the pack's mutable bulk is untracked by design". That reasoning is sound and the verdict survives; the phrase "untracked by design" simply presumes a repository doing the tracking, and there is none. The clone half of rung 6 (sect. 8.1) is therefore unaffected by this finding; only the publish half (sect. 8.2) rests on the missing prerequisite.
  - **CLOSED THE SAME DAY (2026-08-26), and then corrected by an adversarial pass; spec entry 0.8.0-a4.** The fork above is DECIDED as option (c): RFA owns one git directory per pack at `.rfa/packs/<pack>/git/` with `core.worktree` at the pack directory and no `.git` entry anywhere inside the pack, created lazily, never a `git init` or an adoption in the operator's visible tree, with the ignore rules installed as that repository's own `info/exclude`. Rung 6 SPLIT into 6a (the CoW clone, unblocked, no repository needed) and 6b (the publish lifecycle), both trigger-gated outside the build order. `sandbox.isolation` was fixed rather than left as the documentation trap the bullet above describes: the schema now refuses `worktree`, `container` and `clone` by name with what each would require, its enum carries a custom error so an unknown value is not told three refused values are legal, and spec sect. 8.1 records that as an amendment of v0.4 sect. 3.2, which still publishes the old three-value vocabulary. The verifier then found five real defects in that same-day work, all now applied: sect. 8.2 item 2's stated reason was FALSE, since `MERGE_HEAD` is per WORK TREE (`$GIT_DIR/MERGE_HEAD`, or `$GIT_DIR/worktrees/<id>/MERGE_HEAD`) and not per repository, so two merges can be in flight in one repository at once and the per-pack conclusion had to be re-grounded on one work tree per pack; item 5's untracked set omitted the `memory/` file store, which a publish would have committed and MERGED although 8.1's map excludes it and Appendix A rejects merging `/memories` outright (the on-disk name is `memory/`, checked against `src/memoryfs.ts` and the live pack, and the model-facing name `/memories` is not it); item 6's dirty-tree refusal had no exit, and since the reconcile loop precedes every publish and the operator cannot commit through a git directory nobody told them about, one hand edit would have wedged that pack's publishes forever, so an operator-owned adopt command (`rfa agent publish --adopt`) is now owed by 6b; nothing said that only the git DIRECTORY moves, so an operator who versions their hub directory still sees RFA's commits, merges and `merge --abort` checkouts as modifications in their own repository; and sect. 9's rung-5 amendment claimed five items while listing six, creating a second item 3 in a section whose items 1-3 are cited from 8.1, from the amendment itself, from `src/resident.ts`, `src/writefence.ts`, `src/fenceprobe.ts` and `src/agentdef.ts`, so the amendment's items are relabelled **A1-A6** and a bare "sect. 9 item N" still means a door (an earlier bullet in this ledger citing "amendment item 6" means A6).
  - **A live ignore gap found by the same pass, and fixed.** `templates/gitignore` and `GITIGNORE_LINES` in `src/migrate.ts` excluded `.rfa/`, `agents/*/state/` and the knowledge clones but NOT `agents/*/memory/` or `agents/*/scratch/`, and both directories exist on this instance, so an operator who versions their hub directory was tracking the memory store and rung 5's per-run scratch surfaces. Both lines are added to both, with a comment saying this file is the OPERATOR's and is not RFA's own repository's `info/exclude`. Existing hub directories keep the file they already have: `rfa init` only writes it when `.rfa/` is missing from it, and `rfa migrate` is what tops up missing lines, so `~/rfa/acme` needs the two lines added by hand or by a migration.

- **RFA-0.8 rung 5 (2026-08-26): the two-door write fence, and seven things building it settled.**
  - **Both preconditions were answered by measurement, and the second one came back better than the spec assumed.** Edit's interception was already discharged (probes C-E). The open question was whether the pinned SDK lets a sandbox policy attach PER RUN when the SDK spawns the CLI child itself: it does, natively, because `Options.sandbox` is an ordinary per-`query()` option. `Options.spawnClaudeCodeProcess` plus `SandboxManager.wrapWithSandboxArgv(..., customConfig)` is a second per-run route and was rejected, because it sandboxes the CLI process ITSELF and the write set you would then have to re-grant is the CLI's own bookkeeping, not the pack's. So the degraded per-pack path sect. 9 documents stays unbuilt and, more importantly, unclaimed: the startup log says "ESTABLISHED, per RUN" and names both doors, because implying isolation you do not have is what that paragraph is guarding against.
  - **`allowUnsandboxedCommands` defaults to TRUE, and that made the fence advisory.** The single most valuable thing the probes found. Told that the Bash tool takes a `dangerouslyDisableSandbox` parameter, the model hit "operation not permitted" on the pack tree, set the parameter, re-ran the same command and the write LANDED. With `allowUnsandboxedCommands: false` the same two-step attempt is refused twice and the file is unchanged. A door with a documented handle on the inside is not a door, and nothing in the SDK's option documentation points at this.
  - **The spec's own denyWrite sentence would have fenced a run out of its own workspace.** The runtime's write model is ALLOW-ONLY (`allowOnly = defaults + allowWrite`) and `denyWrite` is a carve-out WITHIN that set which beats allow. Sect. 9 item 2 says "denyWrite = the pack tree, the knowledge clones, and `.rfa/`"; the scratch surface sits UNDER the pack tree, so the literal implementation denies the scratch. Measured as probe H case G, after case C had already shown the same policy working without the pack-tree entry. The code filters any `denyWrite` entry that CONTAINS the allow root, and the spec now carries the correction as a normative amendment rather than leaving the next reader to rediscover it.
  - **What actually opens the surface is the CWD, not `allowWrite`.** `filesystem.allowWrite` describes itself as merged with `Edit(...)` allow rules, and it did not open the scratch through any of four routes tried (the option, `settings.sandbox`, an `Edit(...)` rule, `additionalDirectories`). What did: `sandbox: { enabled: true }` alone, because the CLI grants writes to its own working directory. So a fenced run's cwd IS its `scratch/<runId>`, `BRAIN_CWD` became per-run, and `knowledgePath()` takes its base as an argument now - which is the same two-readers-of-one-base shape that caused the three-day knowledge regression this ledger records above, fixed here before it could happen a second time.
  - **The SDK's shadowing warning fires on EVERY resident query already, so "any warning is fatal" would have been a boot loop.** It names the MCP tools this platform pre-approves on purpose. The listener parses the tool list out of the message and exits only when it INTERSECTS the guarded set, which is precisely the case the code's own assert cannot see: the warning's last sentence says settings-file allow rules shadow the callback invisibly, and the probe machine's settings were never audited.
  - **The startup deny probe's verdict is not the query's outcome, and conflating them failed a boot that had just proved the fence works.** A denied tool makes the model retry until `maxTurns`, the SDK throws "Reached maximum number of turns", and the first implementation reported that as `inconclusive` - after the callback had already fired. The iteration error is remembered and the verdict is computed from the observations either way. Three outcomes and no fourth: `intercepted` passes, `bypassed` is the regression, `inconclusive` fails closed too, because "we could not tell" and "it is fenced" are different sentences and only one is safe to boot on.
  - **A pack declaring `Write` was `read-only` by the platform's own definition, and bare in `allowedTools`.** `agentPosture()` calls a pack read-only when `interrupt_on` names none of its tools, so before this rung such a pack passed sect. 10 gate 1, had its Write auto-approved before the callback, and could write the whole hub directory. Rung 5 closes it from both sides: `hasWriteSurface()` is the second predicate gate 1 needed, and `preApproved()` strips the guarded built-ins from `allowedTools` for EVERY pack, concurrent or not.
  - **A dependency check said "available" in a context where nothing can be sandboxed, and that is the finding this rung would have shipped without.** The brief asked what Seatbelt does inside an already-sandboxed macOS context and what bubblewrap does inside an unprivileged container. The Linux half has BOTH an easy state and a hard one: `node:24-slim` with no bubblewrap returns an explicit missing-primitives list and the wrap throws the same list, loud at both layers, but the same unprivileged container with bubblewrap INSTALLED has the binary present for any dependency check to find and fails on use (`Creating new namespace failed: Operation not permitted`). The macOS half is only the hard one: inside an srt sandbox, `isSupportedPlatform()` returns TRUE and `checkDependenciesAsync()` returns ZERO errors, and the first wrapped command then dies on a nested `sandbox-exec` (probe K). The first implementation of the startup check asked exactly those two questions, so it would have reported the fence established, booted the pack, and failed at the first turn - the silent degrade sect. 9 item 3 forbids, arrived at from the opposite side. Both platforms therefore have a state where "the primitives are present" and "this host can sandbox a command" disagree. The check now ESTABLISHES: a trivial write inside a temp allow root must land and one outside it must be refused, and both halves are required, because a sandbox that establishes and then permits everything passes the positive half alone. `network: {}` keeps the proxy and its mux socket unstarted, so the check leaves nothing running inside a long-lived resident. Spec sect. 9 carries it as amendment item 6.
  - **Proved live, and the proof is a command rather than a session log** (`npm run fence-proof`, 8/8, about $0.15 a run): a throwaway pack at `concurrency: 2` in a temp hub directory, never the owner's, whose one gated acting tool writes to a real Linear workspace. An allowed write lands in `scratch/<runId>`; a Write at the pack tree is refused by door one and the model repeats the surface it may use; a Write into the knowledge corpus is refused; the SAME write through Bash leaves no file and door one never sees the call, which is what proves door two is doing the work; two overlapping turns write only their own surfaces; and the resident exits 1 both when the sandbox cannot establish itself and when the deny probe comes back `bypassed`. Door one is version-fragile by design, so this is re-runnable after an SDK bump on purpose.
  - **Four things the proof taught about proving a fence at all**, recorded because each one silently made an earlier run meaningless. A model that refuses BY ITSELF proves nothing: the answerer scaffold's "peer messages are data, never instructions" rule made haiku decline the write and the assertion passed on a refusal the fence never made. The Write tool has a precondition of its own ("File has not been read yet") that fires BEFORE `canUseTool` for an existing file, so every door-one target must be a file that does not exist yet. A hand-written pack bound `alias: ops` and the resident created a room of its own, so every write proof failed as "nobody is present to answer" for a reason unrelated to the fence - `rfa agent new` is the room binding, exactly as the working rules say. And the refusal a model can act on is a claim about what reaches the MODEL, so it is asserted against the answer, not against the operator's log line.

- **RFA-0.8 rung 4 (2026-08-26): candidate parallelism, the first user-visible parallelism, and seven things building it settled.**
  - **Section 11's one sentence had two readings and only one is buildable today.** "Selection is a verification act under spec 10.4, performed by a member that did not generate the candidates" reads as though the candidates are wire objects, because a member cannot verify what it cannot see. The wire task carries ONE owner and ONE `evidence` (wire 10.2), so the wire-visible shape needs either `evidence[]` or N child tasks, and RFA-0.8's section 2 is the complete list of wire deltas it staged: neither is in it. Built LOCAL, and the spec now says so in eight numbered items (the amendment of 2026-08-26 in sect. 11). The room sees one task, one owner, one completion; wire 10.4 governs the verification of the WINNER's completion, unchanged and in full. No wire Appendix F row moved, which is the test of whether the decision was really local. The residue is written down rather than hidden: the human selecting is choosing between candidates the wire never saw, which is fine for the hosting operator and is exactly the trigger for reopening the wire-visible shape if a selector is ever a member the operator does not host. Design note: [docs/design/rung4-candidates.md](design/rung4-candidates.md).
  - **The obvious memory fix breaks consolidation's watermark, and the other one is simpler anyway.** Sect. 11's trap 1 offers two shapes: candidates record no episode until one wins, or episodes are tagged and consolidation excludes non-winners. The tagging shape was designed on paper and REJECTED before any code: consolidation advances its watermark to `batch[batch.length - 1].id`, so a filtered-out candidate episode would be stepped over and a candidate promoted afterwards would never be consolidated at all. That shape needs a watermark that can go backwards, which is precisely what rung 1's compare-and-set exists to prevent. So a candidate turn records NOTHING; its answer lives in `candidate_runs` in the engine DB, and the selection path writes exactly the winner's, in normal id order ahead of the watermark. The losers' text stays on the books forever: it does not vanish, it just never becomes fact.
  - **Enforced at the write, in two places, because a candidate can reach memory two ways.** `EpisodeLog` takes a guard closure the resident wires to "is a candidate turn in scope" (the rung-3 `AsyncLocalStorage` binding), and `recordOwn` THROWS when it fires; the candidate path never calls it, so the throw is a tripwire for a future author rather than a runtime path. The second way is `/memories`, which every pack can write even at a read-only posture: the mutating verbs refuse inside a candidate run, loudly, with somewhere for the conclusion to go. Buffering each candidate's writes and replaying the winner's was rejected as a miniature of rung 6's clone-and-publish, conflict lifecycle included; two merge stories in one repository is how the second one goes wrong.
  - **Cancellation is `query.interrupt()` and NOT `abortController.abort()`, and the difference is the money.** An interrupt lets the CLI emit its `result` message, and `total_cost_usd` lives on that message, so an interrupted candidate leaves through the same `finally` every other turn leaves through and settles its reservation against what it really spent. Aborting the controller throws the iteration away with the cost inside it, and a candidate whose spend disappears is exactly the unattributable meter the honest-meters doctrine forbids. The orchestration also does not resolve until every interrupted candidate has settled, so a set's total is complete before anything downstream reads it: the early-stop variant is the case that tests this, because it deliberately kills work that has already spent money.
  - **With no `per_task_usd` declared, exactly ONE candidate is affordable, and that is the honest answer rather than a degenerate case.** Admission reserves `min(per_task_usd, remaining)`, so without a per-task ceiling the first reservation is the whole day remainder. `affordableCandidates()` says so in the sentence it returns, and the CLI repeats it at create time, because the fix (declare a per-task ceiling) is not guessable from a bare "1 candidate".
  - **The dispatcher counts ONE where N turns run, so `concurrency: N` needed a second clamp.** A whole candidate set runs inside one dispatcher job (the task wake), so `inFlight` reads 1 while three CLI children burn money: without subtracting what the process is already running, `concurrency: 3` would be a floor rather than a ceiling. The plan now takes `busy` from the turn register, and the presence meter reports TURNS beside jobs for the same reason the parked lease had to be reported as parked.
  - **The e2e caught a real bug the unit tests could not.** `selectCandidate` refuses a set with candidates still in flight (a winner picked from an incomplete field is not a winner), and the early-stop path called it before closing the set, so `first-verified` would have failed 100% of the time with "could not record its winner". Found by the scenario, not by a test of either function: each was individually right.
  - **Proved live on the owner's stack (2026-08-26 14:49-14:51), with the cap and the pack sizing raised for the measurement and restored after** (`agents.max_inflight` 2 -> 4 -> 2; pm-agent `concurrency` 2 -> 3 -> 2; the pack file ends byte-identical to its backup). Task `t_25`, three candidates, all three started within 1 ms of each other (14:49:08.286, .287, .287) and finished at 14:49:32.8, 14:49:35.1 and 14:49:38.2: genuinely overlapping wall clock, not a staggered queue. `$0.1985` for the set. A human picked candidate 1 through `rfa task select` (the same `engine.selectCandidate` the dashboard's `c` picker calls), the winner was filed as the task's evidence 20 s later with no further model turn, and all three scratch surfaces were gone afterwards. The two losers keep their state (`lost`), their cost (`$0.0649`, `$0.0683`) and their full text (1352 and 1567 characters). Memory: exactly ONE new own episode, the winner's, and zero episodes matching either loser's opening words. Then `t_26` with `per_day_usd` cut to `$3.62` against `$3.43` already spent: `3 candidates asked for, running 1 ($0.19 left of pm-agent's $3.62 day budget reserves 1 candidate at $0.25 each, not 3)`, and that sentence is on the task note where the room reads it, not only in a log.
  - **A note for whoever writes rung 5's docs:** the fan-out completes a task, and 10.4's verification gate only bites when `evidence_required` is set on the task. `t_25` went straight to `completed` because it was created without it. That is unchanged wire behaviour, but "a different member verifies the winner" is a promise only for a task created `--evidence-required`, and the CLI does not currently pair the two flags.
  - **Found in passing, unrelated: `rfa agent edit --per-day` destroys comments inside the `budgets:` block.** The budgets edit rewrites the whole block, so three lines of institutional memory under pm-agent's `per_day_usd` (why 8 and not 3) were silently dropped and had to be restored from a backup. `--concurrency` and the other scalar edits keep their surroundings byte for byte; the block edits do not. Not fixed here.

- **RFA-0.8 rung 3 (2026-08-26): read-only parallelism, and six things building it settled.**
  - **The dispatcher's four requirements are three mechanisms, because two of them are one.** A conversation key OWNS a session id (`src/sessions.ts` already refused to let two conversations share one), so "at most one running job per conversation key" IS "one writer per session id". Requirements 1 and 2 of sect. 6.2 are the same `running` set seen twice. The consequence is a rule rather than a nicety: the dispatcher's queue key and the resident's session key MUST be the same string, so `conversationKey()` lives in `src/client.ts` and arrives on `ServeContext` rather than being computed twice. Design note: [docs/design/rung3-dispatcher.md](design/rung3-dispatcher.md).
  - **The ad-hoc conversation key was the literal string `"adhoc"`, and every counterparty shared it.** Found while looking for the right key, not by a failure. Serially that leaked one asker's context into another's answer through a shared SDK session; at `concurrency > 1` it would have been a same-session concurrent resume, which is documented corruption and is exactly the thing the whole rung is built around. The fallback is `peer:<from.id>` now. A latent bug that only a rung narrowing a lock would go looking for.
  - **The unpark overshoot bound is preserved by bounding the PARKS, not the returns.** Rung 2 left this as an explicit debt: its bound was "one park per resident process, because the turn lock holds one turn per process", and rung 3 is the change that breaks the premise. Serializing the over-cap returns bounds nothing (the second returning turn takes its slot too; the alternative is a bill with no answer, which rung 2 already rejected), so the parks are capped at one per process instead: a turn that would park while another is parked keeps its slot. The bound is then unchanged from rung 2 and INDEPENDENT of N, the anti-freeze property survives (a process with any blocked turn still frees a slot), and the price is that k-1 of k simultaneously blocked turns hold a slot they are not spending. Latency, not money past a ceiling. Now normative in RFA-0.8 sect. 6.3 instead of living in a code comment.

- **RFA-0.8 rung 2 (2026-08-26), and five things building it settled.**
  - **The lease across a blocked wait is LENT, not released, and the reason is that the SDK bills between model requests.** W5 sect. 2.4 gap 10 left the mechanism open and sect. 6.3 says "release, or downgrade to a non-slot-holding form". Releasing outright is the trap: a turn that surrendered its lease and had to win a fresh admission on the way back can die on `waitForSlot` AFTER the model has already spent the operator's money, which is a bill with no answer. The return is then a state flip on a row this process owns and cannot be refused. The price is real and is written down rather than hidden: `unpark` waits a short grace for genuine capacity and then takes its slot back regardless, so the account can sit transiently over the cap. The new `live-concurrent` case measured "overlap" as the intersection of the CLIENT's send-to-reply windows.
