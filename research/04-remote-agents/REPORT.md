## TL;DR

**The premise wins on shape and loses on scope, and the most valuable output of this wave is a bug list against the product that exists today.** Six dimensions found, in shipped code that only local agents touch: `/mcp` has no transport authentication of any kind (`src/main.ts` routes everything but `/auth` and `/api/*` straight to the MCP handler, so `room_create` is an uncredentialed resource-creation primitive); `history_visibility: "joined_after"` is enforced only when building the join contract (`src/store.ts:1516` checks `since > room.seq` and nothing else), so a joiner reads up to 200 prior events including every message body; the policy gate has exactly one call site (`src/store.ts:846`, inside `send`), so task `note`, `description` and `evidence.summary` reach a human's approval card and a resident's prompt uninspected; `verify` authorizes any member whose id differs from the owner (`src/store.ts:1722`), so one principal holding two memberships accepts its own evidence and an unbounded rejection loop wedges the board; quarantine keys on attacker-chosen `name` OR card digest (`src/store.ts:437`, faithful to spec 12.1, so it is a spec defect first); `writeMeta` is a non-atomic whole-file `fs.writeFileSync` (`src/store.ts:2199`) whose torn output makes `loadFromDisk` skip an entire room with one stderr line; send idempotency consults only an in-memory map reset on load; a claimed task is never released by any code path; and `wrapForModel` (`src/client.ts:451`) does not call `neutralize`, so raw control characters, bidi overrides and the Unicode TAG block reach the model verbatim. **None of those needed the premise change to be worth fixing.**

**One hub-derived field settles the concept war.** Five dimensions invented four names for the same fact: `zone: local|remote` [05], `home` (opaque org label) [06], verified `provider.origin` (a domain) [01], `org` on a delegation chain [03]. Ship exactly one: **`home`**, an opaque label derived at admission from the peer's admission record, stamped on the member record, the presence record, roster snapshots and `envelope.from`. `home === "local"` yields every boolean predicate [05] wants; a boolean cannot yield a label, which is the lesson Slack paid for when `is_shared` had to grow `is_ext_shared` and `is_org_shared` [06]. Supervisability is a genuinely separate fact (your own LangGraph process on your own VPS is accountable-local and unrestartable) and it does **not** go on the wire: the hub already knows whether its own supervisor owns a process.

**T1 was two orthogonal problems wearing one name, which is why the notes read as contradictory.** Separate them permanently: **credential issuance** is local and always local (a hub-minted single-use invite now, a guest client in the operator's own IdP later, never the peer's IdP token, which MCP 2026-07-28 does forbid a resource server from accepting), while **identity anchoring** is the peer's key, pinned by RFC 7638 thumbprint at admission and used to verify a card and later a signed result. The correction wave 03 needs is real but narrower than [01] claimed: MCP authorization is verbatim OPTIONAL, the client-credentials **grant** is OAuth 2.1 core (only [01]'s "it is a draft" framing is wrong, the MCP profile of it is the draft), and the wave-03 conclusion that T1 was unsatisfiable rested on confusing the client MUST (send `resource`) with the server MUST (validate `aud`). None of that makes an authorization server inside the hub acceptable. It stays rejected forever.

**Per-message signing unparks in one narrow form and only on demand.** Three dimensions unparked it [01][03][05], two parked it [02][06], and the arguments do not actually conflict: nobody argues for signing chat, and [02]'s stated reason ("the hub is trusted by construction") is false in the exact scenario the premise creates, since the hub operator is a party to any cross-org dispute. Full-envelope signing is structurally impossible (`seq`, `ts`, `from` and `prev_hash` are hub-assigned at `src/store.ts:822-843` and `:2134-2137`) and [01]'s signed-core proposal is unverifiable from the durable record as specified, because it includes a `signed_at` field the stored envelope does not have. What survives: sign the **claim and the result**, put `signed_at` in the JWS protected header, and build it when one peer answers yes to "do you need to prove to a third party what you did or did not send". Until then the honest sentence for spec 13 is that the hash chain proves no party **other than the hub** rewrote the log.

**The scarce resource is not code, it is a counterparty.** Six notes produced roughly forty adopt verdicts, thirty of which touch the wire, and not one named a candidate peer. The first-version wire delta below is five field-and-verb groups; everything else is parked with a trigger. Build the week of work that is locally correct on its own merits and happens to be the cross-org substrate, add a cold-start guest as a standing end-to-end test, and make the next step conditional: **if no named peer exists sixty days after the cold-start guest passes, the answer was "deepen local"** and the remaining thirty recommendations stay parked.

## 1. What the premise actually changes, and what it does not

Three things genuinely change when a member belongs to another organization.

**The operator stops being the only party.** The hash chain (`src/store.ts:2134-2137`, genesis `sha256hex(room.handle)`, a public value) is tamper evidence against a third party and worthless against the party running the process. That is the one genuinely new cryptographic requirement in the wave [01], and it is the argument that narrows signing to the objects a human will act on rather than expanding it to chat.

**The credential stops being shareable.** `src/store.ts:384` mints exactly one `joinSecret` per room and `:433` compares it with `!==`. One leak grants any-name, any-card entry, with no attribution, no per-holder revocation and no expiry. Worse, the durable credential is not the join secret at all: `auth()` (`src/store.ts:2001-2013`) resolves every call from an opaque `membership_token` that never expires, never rotates, travels as an ordinary tool **argument** through every client's logs and transcripts, and restores an offline member's presence rather than rejecting it, so lease expiry does not invalidate it. Spec 4.2 already says tokens SHOULD be short-lived with refresh; that is unimplemented.

**Read access stops being harmless.** This is the finding the wave under-ranked and the completeness pass correctly promoted. `src/store.ts:376` defaults `history_visibility: "member"`, `:110` sets `replayCap: 200`, and `:542-546` replays the last `history_limit` events to any joiner with no per-member visibility predicate. `room_listen{since: 0, wait_for: "all"}` then returns up to 200 more (the cursor jumps to `room.seq` unconditionally at `:1531`, so this is bounded at 200 events and **not** the whole log, contrary to [05]'s headline, but 200 events of a working room is every recent message body, every task, every intervention). `room_task list` returns every task with full `note` and `evidence` to every non-observer (`src/store.ts:1661`). `room_roster` plus `agent_describe` hand over every local member's full card. Admitting one guest today is a bulk disclosure event before anyone has a chance to moderate anything.

What does **not** change: federation is still not required (a remote member is a client of one hub), the room is still the isolation unit, the policy gate is still the right architectural place for per-counterparty content rules, and the platform's storage shape is untouched.

## 2. Identity: issuance is local, anchoring is the peer's domain

**Credential issuance.** The hub mints, the hub revokes. Concretely, an operator-provisioned `deploy/peers.json` holding one record per peer:

```json
{ "peer_id": "p_orgb_worker",
  "home": "orgb.example",
  "name_hint": "orgb-analyst",
  "key_thumbprint": "NzbLsXh8uDCcd-6MNwXF4W_7noWXFZAfHkxZsRGC9Xs",
  "expires_at": "2026-11-17T00:00:00Z",
  "admitted_by": "principal_paul",
  "max_memberships": 2, "member_rpm": 10, "usd_per_day": 1.00 }
```

`room_admin invite` (human-origin only, reusing the existing authority path) mints a single-use expiring `invite_token` bound to a `peer_id`. `room_join` accepts `invite_token` or the legacy `join_secret`; the legacy secret stays legal and SHOULD be restricted to rooms whose members are all local. The membership records `peer_id` and `home`, and **the hub MUST reject a call whose membership was admitted under a different transport principal than the one presented.** That one sentence is the fix for the fatal linkage gap the verifier found in [01]: without it, adding a transport credential creates two independent credentials, and org B holding a perfectly valid scoped token can drive org A's membership the moment it learns A's `membership_token`, which travels as a plain tool argument.

**Identity anchoring.** Pin the peer's RFC 7638 JWK thumbprint in the admission record and resolve card signatures against it. `src/signing.ts` already does JWS over JCS with EdDSA/ES256 and binds `kid` to the thumbprint when a key is embedded, so this is configuration plus a per-peer lookup, not crypto.

**Rejected: admit-by-URL against A2A's well-known card.** [06] recommended fetching `https://{domain}/.well-known/agent-card.json`, validating self-consistency, caching per HTTP headers and refetching on digest change, on the claim that "nothing needs inventing". The verifier killed it and the kill stands: A2A 1.0.0 section 8.4.3 resolves verification keys through `kid` **and `jku`**, which `src/signing.ts` does not implement and which would add a second SSRF surface plus trust-on-first-use; A2A does not restrict algorithms while `src/signing.ts:105` allows only EdDSA and ES256, so a mainstream RS256-with-`jku` card fails outright; and an A2A AgentCard describes an A2A **server** with `interfaces[]` and `securitySchemes`, which an RFA guest (a client of your hub) may not run at all. Paste the card JSON into the console and pin the thumbprint after one out-of-band confirmation. Same property, no fetch path, no SSRF defences, no cache semantics, no dependency on the peer's web server being up when a member needs to rejoin.

**Rejected outright, and named as ceremony so it stops coming back:** an authorization server inside the hub; Dynamic Client Registration; a registry as a trust root (measured: `kenhuangus/ANS` 4 stars last pushed 2025-06-21, NANDA only zero-star third-party prototypes, `agntcy/dir` 175, and the one registry with traction publishes MCP **servers** [06]); DIDs and verifiable credentials; macaroons (HMAC-chained, so every verifier can forge, fatal across orgs), biscuit (solves offline attenuation, which a hub-mediated room does not have), UCAN (DID-addressed), GNAP; IBCTs from AIP, whose own limitations section records no production deployment, localhost-only evaluation, unenforced revocation and self-reported completion blocks [01]; a DNS TXT domain-proof second anchor (zero users, adds unauthenticated DNS to the trust base); SPIFFE/SPIRE as anything but an optional mTLS adapter for an org already running it; and flat verb scopes. On that last one: `scopes[]` appears in the admission record above as a **reserved** field with no enforcement point, because [04]'s and [06]'s scope proposals were circular (a fourth role was rejected *because* scopes exist, and scopes were introduced with no enforcement point named). Role plus `guest_task_verbs` plus the gate expresses every distinction the premise names. Add scopes when a second peer provably needs a different grant than the first.

**On MCP conformance, stated honestly.** The four sentences [01] and [06] quote are verbatim correct ("MCP servers MUST only accept tokens that are valid for use with their own resources", "MUST NOT accept or transit any other tokens", the RFC 8707 audience MUST, and the MUST NOT on passing a client token upstream). The same page opens with "Authorization is **OPTIONAL** for MCP implementations". RFA's `membership_token` is a tool argument, not an OAuth bearer, so nothing RFA does today is non-conformant and nothing is currently forbidden. What is forbidden is the shape people assume for cross-org, namely accepting the peer's own IdP access token. Say that, and do not write "the transport spec forbids it" about a page whose first line is OPTIONAL.

## 3. Interop: the stranger surface exists, and the artifact ships wrong

[02] is the wave's best empirical work: a remote agent joined the standing room, claimed a task, completed it with evidence and got verified, three separate ways (curl alone, `langchain-mcp-adapters` 0.3.2, and a 40-line pure-`httpx` member with no `mcp` dependency). **MCP-over-HTTP is plain JSON POST.** That single fact resolves the REST-binding argument below and means nothing needs inventing on the wire for a stranger to do real work.

The measured frictions are small and specific: the OpenAI Agents SDK defaults `client_session_timeout_seconds = 5`, so `room_listen(timeout_ms=20000)` dies with `McpError: Timed out ... Waited 5.0 seconds`, which reads to a stranger as "RFA is broken" and is one table cell to prevent; `langchain-mcp-adapters` survives to 45s only because the hub answers a legacy-era client with SSE framing governed by `sse_read_timeout`, which is an accident of the dual-era handler and must be recorded with the era it was measured in, never as a per-framework contract; the hub advertises no extensions in `server/discover` although spec 16 describes exactly that document (grep for `extensions` in `src/hub.ts` and `src/main.ts` returns nothing), so a peer cannot negotiate anything; and the MCP v2 SDK returns input-validation failures as plain text, which is the one error class a learner hits most.

**The artifact ships wrong and must not ship that way.** `rfa_min.py` as drafted in [02] returns raw peer envelopes to the caller: no boundary wrapper, no sender-name sanitization. Spec 14.3 is a MUST on client SDKs, so the flagship interop asset would be the wormable default the spec forbids, copied far more often than any SDK is installed. Two changes, and they are the reason this is a design finding and not a doc nit:

1. `rfa_min.py` includes the wrapper and the name allowlist, in eight lines.
2. **The hub offers already-boundaried text.** Add `wrapped` (the `<room-message ...>` rendering) alongside `body` in `room_listen` results, because the one thing you cannot control across an organizational boundary is whether the peer's client does its homework. This is also the answer to the mirror threat model nobody wrote: a hostile reviewer at org B asks what your room does to *their* agent, and "we documented that you should wrap it" is a worse answer than "we hand you the wrapped form".

**The sanitizer character set, and where the fix actually goes.** `neutralize()` (`src/client.ts:562`) strips C0 controls only, which arXiv 2607.05744 measured as exactly the "representative string-matching sanitizer" that 4 of 8 techniques evade, with the Unicode TAG block (U+E0000-U+E007F) invisible in a human approval view while reaching the model verbatim. The correction the verifier forced: `neutralize` is only reached from `sanitizeForMemory`, i.e. the **memory** path. `wrapForModel` does not call it, so the prompt path today passes raw C0 controls too. The fix lands in `wrapForModel` and in the new task-text wrapper, covering C0, bidi controls (U+202A-U+202E, U+2066-U+2069), zero-width and joiners (U+200B-U+200D, U+2060, U+FEFF), the entire TAG block, and whitespace-run folding.

**A2A, decided.** Keep the mappings current (add `TASK_STATE_UNSPECIFIED` and `TASK_STATE_AUTH_REQUIRED` as two rows in spec 10.2; that is the whole cheap half). Reject an A2A **server facade** inside the hub: A2A 1.0 has no delegation, no task forwarding and no multi-party primitive through v1.0.1, so a facade must collapse the roster into one agent, which is Matrix's `bridgebot` pattern that its own docs call a terrible experience, for zero multi-party gain. Build a connector when a named A2A peer exists, never before.

## 4. Remote tasks: a claim is a lease, and self-reports are decoration

Two defects make a remote worker unusable and both are in shipped code.

**A claimed task is never released.** `claim` sets `owner` and `state = "working"` (`src/store.ts:1663-1674`); nothing in `sweep()` looks at task ownership; `task_overdue` appends a system event and mutates nothing (`:1941-1957`); `removeMembership` does not touch owned tasks. So a peer that crashes, or simply leaves, holds the task and every dependent task forever, recoverable only by a supervisor `cancel`.

**A reconnecting worker cannot complete its own work.** Every `room_join` mints a fresh `m_` id (`src/store.ts:497`) and `complete` requires `task.owner === member.id` (`:1704`). The work happens, the result exists, the protocol refuses to record it.

The fix is a lease with a fence, and the fence must not be public. [03] proposed a `claim_id` carried inside the task object, which `emit` appends into the room log verbatim, so every member and observer learns it on the first legitimate use and the proposed "a valid `claim_id` from a new member id re-binds ownership" rule becomes privilege escalation. That is fatal and is not recommended. The shape that works:

- The **event** carries `attempt` (a monotonic integer) and `lease_expires`. Public, orderable, auditable. This is the fence in Kleppmann's sense; a random string cannot be ordered and was never a fence.
- The **claim result only** returns an opaque `claim_token` (>= 16 bytes, tied to the lease), never echoed into any event, never in a roster snapshot.
- `complete`, `update` and `release` accept `claim_token`. A valid token presented from a **new member id** re-binds ownership only when the presenting membership carries the same `peer_id` as the original claimant. A stale token fails with the already-declared, currently-unused `lease_expired` code carrying `{current_attempt, current_owner, task_state}`.
- Release fires on the **existing** offline transition, on `leave`, and on eviction. The sweep already detects offline members and already emits `gone_quiet` (`src/store.ts:1902-1915`), so this is a branch in a loop that runs, not a new timer subsystem. Drop `lease_ttl_s`, the 30..3600 clamp, `task_lease_grace_s` and the three new system events [03] specified: once presence renews the claim (which is right, and free, because presence renewal already happens on every `room_listen`), the only case that ever expires is a member going fully offline.
- `max_attempts` defaults to **1**, and automatic requeue is opt-in per task. [03] proposed 3. A remote task may already have filed a document or moved money in infrastructure the hub cannot see; the honest default for a cross-org side effect is one execution plus a released task a human or another member can pick up.
- Restart grace: after boot, the hub MUST NOT expire a claim for one full lease period. Absence of a heartbeat during hub downtime is the hub's fault. Chubby handles this explicitly and [03]'s "the restart-durability question is already answered there" was a misreading: `reply_by` is an absolute requester SLA that should fire after a restart, a lease is the opposite.
- `max_claims_per_member` (default 3), and mutating `room_task` actions counted against a **separate** task-action budget, not `member_rpm` (which is a message budget whose counter only advances in `send` at `src/store.ts:918`; sharing one window means a worker doing progress plus complete spends the budget it needs to answer a question).

**Evidence, with a verifier that means something.** `verify` today checks only `task.owner !== member.id`, so two memberships from one org accept each other's fabricated evidence, and, worse in the other direction, a single hostile member can `reject` every evidence-bearing completion in the room indefinitely (reject sets state back to `working`, with no cap and no terminal state). Both need positive authorization: the verifier must be a **local member, the task creator, or a human principal**, and `evidence_required` must be forced **at claim time** when the claimer is not `home: "local"` (setting it at create time cannot work: the flag is fixed before any owner exists, so the default path leaves `complete` terminal with no verifier at all). [03]'s "verifier must be local" also needs the honest caveat that it re-imports the single-operator assumption for a room with two remote orgs and no human present; hence creator-or-human as alternatives.

**The rule that makes the rich result object safe, and it belongs in the spec verbatim:** everything a peer reports about its own execution (`cost`, `tool_trace`, progress percentages, `verification_status: self_reported`) is untrusted decoration, MUST be rendered as self-reported, and MUST NOT be an input to any automated decision. Only a local, creator or human verifier changes state. Without that sentence [03]'s structured result becomes a set of fields the console quietly presents as truth.

**Rejected:** zkML proofs of remote inference (zkLLM needs about 15 GPU-minutes for one 13B inference and proves committed weights over an input, not the tool calls nobody can see); TEE and RFC 9334 attestation (proves which code ran, never that the answer is right; the roles do not even fit, since nobody holds a reference measurement of a correct answer); economic bonds and staking (the verifier's dilemma: when verification is expensive, nobody re-executes, so the penalty never fires); reputation scores (EigenTrust needs many raters and scarce identity; a five-member room has neither); quorum or redundant execution as the trust mechanism (the 2026 Knight-Leveson replication with coding agents measured 429 coincident failures where independence predicts 115.36, a 3.7x excess, so agreement is weak evidence); a delegation chain with `max_depth` and per-hop `context` (this wave creates no hops, RFA's `parent_id` is sub-task decomposition inside one board, and wave 03 already designed the chain as part of the parked `handoff` verb with `max_depth` default 2, so [03]'s version silently raises a parameter for a feature with no consumer); a cost ledger in the hub; contract-net auction verbs (and note the "award" case [05] wanted already works: `create` accepts `owner`, and a task with a pre-set owner cannot be claimed).

## 5. Deployment: one hub per org, with the topologies named

**One hub per org, and remote agents are guests on it.** [04] reached this by finding that the workbench is hub-scoped by construction (`hub.pendingApprovals()` iterates every room; `/api/agents/<n>/definition` serves any pack's system prompt; `/api/runs` and `/api/summary` have no room filter), so one leaked 12-hour session token reads every room's pending approvals and every agent prompt. That is correct as a conclusion but over-determined as an argument, because [04]'s own recommendations 6 and 7 fix exactly that. Name the three topologies instead, and put them in the spec:

1. **One org's hub with guests.** The product. The operator is a party, which is why signatures are the price of this topology rather than a maturity milestone.
2. **A neutral third-party hub where the operator is not a party.** The only topology in which the hash chain is credible to both sides without signatures, and the shape any future hosted offering would take. Not built, named.
3. **Two hubs federated.** Rejected. See section 7.

**Packaging.** Ship a `Dockerfile` and a `compose.yaml` (named volume for `/data`, `restart: unless-stopped`, `healthcheck` against `/healthz`, supervisor `depends_on` hub `condition: service_healthy`). Document "run it under systemd or launchd" in three lines and stop there. [04] recommended a systemd unit with `LoadCredentialEncrypted=` on top of the retained launchd plists, which is four boot paths for one developer, and the secrets rationale is wrong: the exposure it cites is the resident **children's** `/proc/<pid>/environ`, written by `src/supervisor.ts:79-93`, and systemd credentials are by design not inherited by child processes, so they do not reach it. `LoadCredentialEncrypted` also binds ciphertext to the host TPM, breaking the copy-the-unit-and-restore-elsewhere property that STATUS's rehearsed restore depends on.

**Durability, all pre-existing and all cheap.** `writeMeta` (`src/store.ts:2199`) is an in-place whole-file write of a 49 KB snapshot and the loader swallows a parse failure with `console.error("skipping corrupt room file")`, so a crash mid-write silently deletes a room's members, tasks, approvals, quarantine set and join secret while the NDJSON log sits on disk unloaded. Fix with temp file plus `fs.renameSync` **plus `fsync` of the temp fd and the directory**, and do **not** adopt [04]'s "refuse to boot" remedy: tasks, approvals, quarantine and epoch are all derivable from the log (task events carry the full task object, roster events carry `rosterSnapshot`), so the correct degradation is to rebuild from the log and treat meta as a cache, which costs everyone a rejoin instead of costing the room its existence. The same window exists in the other direction: `emit` appends the task event **before** `writeMeta`, and `loadFromDisk` restores tasks only from `meta.tasks`, so a crash in that gap silently reverts a winning claim while the claim event stays in the chain, and a second peer can then win the same task. That breaks the "exactly one claimant wins" guarantee `src/hub.ts:344` advertises, and it is the fourth room-store defect the wave did not name.

Send idempotency consults only `room.dedupe`, reset to `new Map()` on load (`src/store.ts:2225`), while `sentIds` **is** rebuilt from the log (`:2271`) and never consulted. State the fix honestly: checking `sentIds` can only return a degraded result, because `SendResult.recipients` is computed at send time and never persisted, so the spec sentence must say what a replayed result contains rather than promising the original. Also note `sentIds` is capped at 500 per member (`:917`) and `dedupe` at 2000 (`:965`), so "the window survives as long as the log retains the message" is stronger than either implementation.

**Rejected on evidence:** managed serverless (and state the surviving reason, since Cloud Run now supports persistent volume mounts and configurable min-instances: SQLite WAL "does not work over a network filesystem" and every mountable volume is one, plus a revision bringing up a second instance breaks the exclusive `.hub.lock`); Kubernetes; a multi-process hub behind a load balancer (waiters and watchers are process-local); a Node SEA single binary (Stability 1.1, and `postject`-built addons crash on `process.dlopen()` on Linux arm64); Homebrew as the primary channel; and the **`node:sqlite` port**, whose own gate fails: official Node builds compile `node:sqlite` without `SQLITE_ENABLE_FTS5`, `src/memoryfs.ts:246` needs FTS5, and `node:sqlite` does not exist on the Node 20 this repo declares. The single real win from that recommendation survives at a few lines: `better-sqlite3` already exposes `db.backup()`, so delete the `sqlite3` CLI shell-out at `src/platform.ts:38`. Postgres stays parked, with [04]'s correction to the reason: LangGraph's enums match `src/engine.ts:17-19` character for character but the column set does not, and the port is synchronous-to-asynchronous across 56 prepared statements and 5 transactions plus an FTS5 rewrite, for 64 KB plus 225 KB of data.

**The store lockfile** reads, then `process.kill(pid, 0)`, then writes with no `O_EXCL` (`src/store.ts:320-349`). PID liveness is meaningless across containers and PID namespaces, and two racing starts can both pass. Add `O_EXCL` creation plus a heartbeat timestamp in the lock body.

## 6. Hostile peers, both directions, and the data question nobody asked

[05] is strong and one-directional: it models "hostile peer attacks our hub" completely and never models "what does your room do to my agent". Both halves need spec text.

**Inbound, in order of severity.** (1) The join-time and `room_listen` disclosure of section 1, fixed by forcing `history_visibility: "joined_after"` for any member whose `home !== "local"` and clamping `since` to the member's stored `joinedAtSeq` (a new persisted field; `joinSeq` at `src/store.ts:530` is a local variable today, and existing memberships need a migration decision, which is to grant them the room's current tip). (2) The gate's single call site: route mutating `room_task` actions through `evaluateGate` and cap task field sizes (`maxInlineBytes` is checked only against `args.body`, so task text is unbounded and `room_task` is outside the rate window entirely, which makes it simultaneously an egress bypass around every outbound control, an ungated injection path, and a disk-and-RAM growth vector on a hub that holds the event log in memory). Two implementation facts the "it is a call site, not a design" framing hides: `task()` is synchronous while `evaluateGate` is `async`, and the matcher plus the command-check contract are envelope-shaped (`src/store.ts:1085-1112` pipes the envelope JSON to a subprocess), so extending to task actions needs a versioned check-input contract and a defined `hold`-to-`refuse` degradation, because `deploy/gate.json`'s `hold-marker` rule matches on `text_regex` alone and would land a task in an undefined state. (3) Quarantine keyed on `peer_id`, not on name or digest, and revoking the admission record as the real kill switch. (4) Per-peer caps: `member.rateWindow` and `bodyHashes` are re-initialized on every join (`:522-523`), so leave-and-rejoin resets the rate limit with no restart required, and nothing caps memberships per peer, so one peer multiplies its own allowance up to `max_members` (default 32) and can also exhaust the room so nobody else can join. Budgets key on `peer_id`. (5) The approval card: any member can register an approval by riding `ext["io.github.pbeneteau/approval"]` on a send with no role or origin check (`src/store.ts:748-778`), the preview is a raw 200-character slice with no neutralization, and the console renders neither origin nor `home`.

On that last one, [05]'s "the hub MUST generate the action label" is not implementable: the approval ext is opaque to the hub, which executes nothing and does not know what tool the requester intends to call. The implementable version: **require the approval ext to carry a structured `{tool_name, input_preview}`** mirroring the Claude Code channels contract, have the hub stamp requester id, origin, `home` and room, sanitize and length-cap with a counted elision marker, and let the consuming client render requester strings as data. Likewise the provenance block [03] wanted on any card "whose params derive from a remote task result" cannot be computed: the derivation is laundered through a model and there is no taint tracking. The coarse version that **is** derivable from data already recorded: list every non-local member whose content entered the run's context window, from the checkpoint's `room_cursor`.

**Outbound, the mirror.** The hub promises a guest: what it logs (everything, forever, hash-chained), who can read it (the operator and every other member of that room), that the operator can hold, edit and inject messages mid-flight, and that the guest's own text may be quoted into a human approval card. From the peer's side, mid-flight editing is an integrity-of-my-agent's-speech problem, and the spec should say plainly that RFA rooms give the hub operator plaintext by design and that a counterparty who cannot accept that should not hold a membership on someone else's hub. That is also the honest form of the MLS rejection: the party with a confidentiality interest is the counterparty, which does not run the hub, and the rejection stands on architecture (the pre-delivery gate, origin stamping, the chain, moderation holds and the console all require plaintext), not on "the operator does not need to hide content from itself".

**Data protection, the dimension the wave never had.** `grep -i 'retention\|erasure\|redact\|personal data' spec/RFA-0.1.md` returns exactly one line (spec 550). The moment a guest is in a room, the log is a bilateral data store on your disk, in your nightly backups (`~/Backups/rfa-agent-com/<date>`, 7 days), replicated into `data/obs.db`, the episode log and possibly a resident's consolidated fact store. Three answers are needed and two of them are one day of work:

- **A redaction event.** Matrix solved this a decade ago and five notes cite Matrix for keys, room versions, ACLs and bridging without noticing it. `room_admin redact {seq | message_id, reason}` appends a `system {event: "redacted", refs: {target_seq, reason, by}}` event and blanks the body **in the snapshot and in future replay**, leaving the original event and its `prev_hash` link intact so the chain still verifies and who-said-what-when stays answerable at the metadata level. Append-only is preserved; the content is gone from every future read. This is nearly impossible to retrofit into an operator's expectations after the first incident.
- **Retention and exit.** State the room's retention window in the join contract, and give a leaving peer a one-command export of the events it sent and received. `npm run verify-log` (below) is the same code path.
- **The controller/processor question.** The spec cannot answer it, but it must not be silent. Two acceptable positions, and the operator picks one per room: either the room MUST NOT carry personal data (which for the dogfood tenant, an AMF-regulated fintech, means the premise cannot be exercised there without a DPA and third-party-risk review that is a project, not an afternoon), or a written agreement exists before `room_admin invite` is called, and the admission record's `admitted_by` is the audit anchor for it.

**Release obligations, forced rather than deferred.** You cannot tell a stranger org to run this hub while the repo is private with no CI, no `SECURITY.md`, no disclosure contact and no supported-version statement. STATUS records CI as explicitly declined during the research phase; the premise change reopens it. The minimum before any external peer: `SECURITY.md` with a contact and a disclosure window, a supported-version line, and CI running `npm test` plus `npm run e2e`. That is a decision for the owner, and this report's job is to make it explicit rather than let it be discovered by a peer's security review.

**Rejected:** per-skill per-zone card projection (invocation is not gated on skill ids anywhere: the envelope has no skill field and spec 6.3's projection is a client-side naming convention, so a guest that cannot see a skill just sends a plain request and gets the same answer, while serving a filtered card breaks both `card_verified` and the advertised digest); a probation member state with two new admin verbs and two policies (the existing `held` state already blocks sends and task mutations, keeps reads working, is visible in presence and is releasable only by host or supervisor: the small-team version is "hold a new peer until you have seen it do one thing correctly"); an `egress_bytes_per_hour` policy (bytes are a poor proxy for the asset, since the fees and thresholds worth stealing are tens of bytes each, and the coarse control already exists: scope `member_rpm` per `(sender, recipient home)`); metadata-inference defences; and a behavioural watchdog until a corpus of normal cross-org behaviour exists.

**The money, which no dimension proposed.** A guest can address a local resident and burn the operator's model budget indefinitely, and RFA already records USD per run in `data/obs.db`. A per-peer daily USD ceiling (`usd_per_day` in the admission record, checked by the resident at pickup against the same ledger v0.5.2 built) is the cheapest real containment in this wave, and it is built on data that already exists.

## 8. What the protocol must gain as normative wire text

Countable on one hand, because every field is something a stranger must learn and every peer must implement, and [02]'s per-room version pin means each addition freezes older rooms. Target protocol **0.1.8**.

**New fields.**

1. `home` (string, opaque, hub-derived, never client-supplied) on the member record, the presence record (spec 7.1), roster snapshots, and `envelope.from` (spec 8). `"local"` is reserved for the hub's own organization and is the default for any principal that presented no invite, so an upgrading hub does not lock out its own residents. Derive the label from the admission record, not from console free text, and expose a display alias separately.
2. Task fields `attempt` (integer), `lease_expires` (ISO or null) and `released_at`; `claim_token` in the **claim result only**, never in an event or a roster snapshot.
3. Approval ext gains structured `{tool_name, input_preview}` plus hub-stamped `{requester_id, origin, home, room}`; `input_preview` is sanitized and length-capped with a counted elision marker.
4. `room_listen` and the join contract gain `wrapped` alongside `body`: the hub's own `<room-message ...>` rendering, so a peer client cannot omit the boundary.

**New verbs (both on `room_admin`, both human-origin, no new authority path).**

5. `invite {peer_id, ttl_s}` returning `{invite_token, expires_at, uses: 1}`; `room_join` accepts `invite_token`.
6. `redact {seq | message_id, reason}`.

**New event types.** `system {event: "admitted", refs: {member, peer_id, home, kid, method, card_digest, admitted_by, invite_id}}`; `system {event: "task_released", refs: {task_id, attempt, reason}}`; `system {event: "redacted", refs: {target_seq, reason, by}}`.

**New and newly-used error codes.** `invite_invalid` (expired, consumed or unknown); `lease_expired` (declared in spec 15 since 0.1.x and thrown nowhere, now the stale-fence error, carrying `{current_attempt, current_owner, task_state}`); `bad_request` (already thrown by `src/store.ts`, missing from the spec 15 list).

**Normative rules that add no fields and cost nothing.**

- The hub MUST reject a call whose membership was admitted under a different transport principal than the one presented. (The linkage rule; without it a transport credential is decoration.)
- Under `history_visibility: "joined_after"`, `room_listen` MUST clamp `since` to the member's join sequence. `joined_after` is the REQUIRED default for any member whose `home !== "local"`.
- Verification of a stored event resolves the signing key valid at that event's `ts`, with a stated clock-skew tolerance and a hub-side per-`(peer_id, kid)` invalidation timestamp so rotation and compromise are distinguishable. Hub `ts` is authoritative; a sender `signed_at` outside the skew window is stale.
- Everything a peer self-reports about its own execution is untrusted decoration and MUST NOT be an input to an automated decision.
- Quarantine keys on the admission record, not on name or capability digest. This edits **spec 12.1**, which currently mandates the evadable key, so a fix in `src/store.ts` alone would leave the spec telling other implementers to build the same hole.
- Spec 13 gains the scope qualifier: the hash chain proves that no party **other than the hub** rewrote the log.
- The hub MUST obtain its own upstream credential and MUST NOT forward a peer's token (written into Appendix D before passthrough is ever designed).
- Client SDK obligations (spec 9.5) gain bounded-jitter retry for idempotent reads and honoring `retry_after_s`; the hub returns 503 with `Retry-After` during drain. No new error code needed (`retry_after_s` already rides every error).

**Explicitly implementation detail, not wire:** scopes; a fourth role; a separate roster section for guests; probation as a member state; `guest_card` and `guests` room policies; `egress_bytes_per_hour`; per-peer USD ceilings (they live in `deploy/peers.json` and the resident's budget check); `/healthz` (public body `{"ok":true}` only, detail behind the session token, because on a hub other orgs dial into the detailed body is a version banner and an internal-state oracle); `PRAGMA user_version` migrations; the Docker and compose artifacts; a `remote` conformance profile (conformance profiles are for a multi-implementer ecosystem, and RFA has one implementation).

## 9. Competitive read and build-versus-wait

**What is already occupied.** A2A owns cross-org invocation (25,378 stars measured 2026-08-17, Linux Foundation governance, shipped in Azure AI Foundry, Bedrock AgentCore and Google Cloud) and owns signed agent cards, using the same JWS-over-JCS construction `src/signing.ts` already implements. MCP owns credentials: Client ID Metadata Documents deprecating DCR, a stable Enterprise-Managed Authorization extension using ID-JAG, and a draft client-credentials extension. Anthropic shipped the local case **in the same month as this research**: Claude Code cross-session messaging (v2.1.224, `ListAgents`/`SendMessage`) plus agent teams with mailboxes at `~/.claude/teams/{team}/inboxes/{agent}.json`, a shared task list with file-locked claiming and automatic dependency unblocking, and `crossSessionInbound = accept|hold|refuse`.

**What that convergence means, in both directions.** It validates RFA's safety model: Anthropic independently landed origin stamping, no-authority-from-text, a pre-delivery classifier, hold and refuse, rate limiting plus duplicate drop plus a pending cap, and task-not-transcript handoff. It also means **do not build local agent-to-agent messaging as the product**, because that surface is same-principal by construction today (OS-user-restricted sockets, cross-machine only through the user's own Remote Control, one team per session).

**The honest one-year risk.** If Anthropic's boundary moves from "one principal, one machine" to "any principal", the messaging half of RFA is redundant within a year, and this very session's tool list already contains cross-machine and cloud `SendMessage`. If A2A adds a multi-party primitive, the room half is contested too; its roadmap as fetched (last updated 2026-03-10) lists 1.0, extensions, an Inspector, a TCK, six SDKs, best practices and governance, with **no** item for registries, multi-party conversations, delegation or handoff, so the risk is real but not imminent. What survives every one of those moves is the **audit half**: a hash-chained room log across organizations, a human-origin approval verb in the tool-call path, evidence with a non-owner verifier, and presence as an honest liveness contract for processes you cannot restart. Nobody credible is competing for that sentence, which is also the reason the strategic move nobody proposed is the cheapest thing in this wave: **make the audit artifact the demo.** A verifiable log export plus the approval trail for one real task, handed to a prospective peer, is one script (`npm run verify-log`) plus a page.

**Wait, do not build:** an agent directory, a card format, a credential format, an identity provider, a second long-running-task RPC, group encryption, and a registry. **Build, because nobody will:** per-peer admission with revocation, the claim lease, evidence with a real verifier, the redaction event, and the verifiable log export.

## 10. Where the evidence was thin, stated rather than laundered

- **No named counterparty exists.** Six dimensions, zero candidate peers. Every claim about what a peer needs, whether it speaks MCP, whose IdP it uses, and whether it will hold a signing key is inference. [06]'s "requiring MCP client-hood is the main adoption blocker" is asserted, not measured, and its own highest-value spike was left unrun.
- **OWASP page-numbered quotations are unverified.** The 2026 Agentic Applications top ten is a PDF behind a download form; the researcher plainly had it (the ASI04 and ASI08 title corrections check out independently) but no page-numbered verbatim quote was independently confirmed. Do not put one in the spec without re-extracting.
- **The memory-poisoning detector was retracted by its own authors.** arXiv 2606.30566 v1's 0.9563/0.9904 AUC figures are real; v2 (2026-07-21) adds a preregistered follow-up finding "100% false positives conditional on recall_before_send=1" and concludes the signature is "a valid attack precondition, not a maliciousness predicate". Logging the retrieval set is still worth doing, on **forensics** grounds only. Do not promise a detector.
- **MINJA's headline numbers were not verified** (the paper, authors and NeurIPS 2025 venue were). The structural argument stands: the MemoryGate is a cross-sender near-duplicate detector, and a memory-injection attack that works through the agent's own paraphrase is low-similarity by construction, and `retrieve()` ignores `source_origin` although the column exists and is set correctly.
- **Chubby's "typically one minute" lock delay** could not be read this session (the mirrored PDF returned unreadable binary), and it was the sole justification for a 60-second grace default. Pick a number and write it down rather than citing an unread source.
- **A2A's 1.0 release date and its "150+ organizations" figure** were not fetched; the specification content and the star count were.
- **Zitadel, Auth0, Ory Hydra and Cognito behaviour on RFC 8707 and the `act` claim** was not fetched. Treat "check that `aud` actually lands in the token" as a mandatory acceptance test for whichever IdP is ever chosen, not an assumption. Ory Hydra issue 3441 (no audience in client-credentials tokens) is unverified against current versions.
- **[02]'s wire measurements are uncommitted.** The three stranger clients, the byte counts, the timing rows and the quarantine-evasion rejoin all live in a session scratchpad. The code paths make them near-certain (quarantine evasion follows directly from `src/store.ts:437`), but nothing in the repo corroborates a number. Commit the spike scripts under `research/04-remote-agents/spikes/` before any of it is cited in normative text.
- **One fabricated quotation was found and must not propagate:** [02] presents an RFC-2119-capitalized MCP extension-fallback rule as verbatim. MCP's actual wording is "needs to either fall back ... or reject the request with an appropriate error **if the extension is mandatory**" and "it's a good practice to document expected fallback behavior". Adopt the rule as RFA's own MUST; do not attribute the wording to MCP.
- **Teams' "Guest" badge means the opposite of the intended sense** (a B2B guest your own tenant issued). "Guest" is still the right user-facing word for RFA because the hub issues the credential, but the closer Teams analogue is External-Familiar, and the citation must not be offered as support.
- **Line numbers drift.** The notes cite commit `02025de`; HEAD is `48750d9`. Every anchor sampled during verification still resolves within one to three lines, but pin the commit when quoting.

## 11. Spikes: the cheap experiments that settle what is left

1. **Half a day, and it is the acceptance harness for the whole wave.** Run the peer yourself as a genuinely separate org: a second container, its own domain and key, its own LangGraph process, **no repo access and no shared filesystem**, driven by nothing but `INTEROP.md`. **Proves** the coordination cost six notes could not measure from inside the repo, and becomes the standing end-to-end guard against the local-assumption drift that produced the reconnect lockout and the history bypass.
2. **1 hour, free, and it settles a third of the park list.** Put seven questions in writing to two prospective peers: what must you be able to prove to a third party, whose IdP issues your credentials, can your runtime hold a 20-second POST, will you hold a signing key, who signs the data agreement, what may we log about your agent, who pays. **Proves** whether signatures, REST, T1 and webhooks have a consumer. This is a question for humans, not research.
3. **20 minutes.** `send message_id=X`, restart the hub, resend `X`, count message events in `data/rooms/<room>.ndjson`. **Proves** the restart-idempotency defect and what a replayed `SendResult` can honestly contain.
4. **1 hour.** Patch a delay into `writeMeta` behind an env var, `kill -9` mid-write, restart, watch the room vanish; repeat with temp file plus rename plus fsync; then repeat the same trick between `emit`'s append and `writeMeta` and confirm a winning claim reverts. **Proves** both room-store windows.
5. **30 minutes.** Room with `history_visibility: joined_after`, 10 messages, second member joins with `history_limit: 0`, then `room_listen{since: 0, timeout_ms: 0, wait_for: "all"}`. **Proves** the disclosure bug and produces the regression test. Expect up to `replayCap` events, not the whole log.
6. **1 hour.** Put a Unicode TAG-block payload plus a bidi override in a message body and in a task `note`; read the stored episodes, the model-facing wrapped form and the console rendering. **Proves** that `wrapForModel` is the place the fix goes, and builds the regression test.
7. **2 hours.** Two processes claim one task; let the owner go offline; confirm release plus `task_released`; then have the original worker complete with a stale `claim_token`. **Proves** `lease_expired` plus principal-bound re-binding, and that the error data lets a client recover without a human.
8. **Half a day, highest information value in the memory half.** Script a remote member asking a sequence designed to make a resident write its own paraphrase of a false fact, let consolidation run, probe retrieval. **Proves** whether the MemoryGate is blind to the MINJA shape (prediction: the fact lands, tagged `agent`, gate silent) and whether trust-weighted retrieval is worth the multiplier. Tracked as an eval case either way.
9. **1 hour.** Replay the existing room log through the gate with a synthetic non-local `home` label and the two draft guest rules. **Proves** how many holds a real day would produce, and prevents shipping a gate that holds everything.
10. **1 hour.** `npm run verify-log data/rooms/<room>.ndjson` recomputing the chain from genesis and reporting the first divergence, then edit one byte and re-run. **Proves** the audit artifact, which is also the demo.
11. **1 hour.** Add the transport bearer behind a flag, register the hub in Claude Code with `--header Authorization`, run `npm test`, `npm run e2e` and the parity gate. **Proves** whether a real MCP host can carry a static header, and if it cannot, the credential has to move into the tool arguments instead.
12. **2 hours.** Run the hub image behind `tailscale serve` or nginx and log the `Origin` and `Host` actually seen. **Proves** the allowlist config shape, which wave 03 flagged as unmeasured and which 403s the first request if guessed wrong.

## 12. The ladder

Six rungs, each independently shippable and live-verifiable in days, each behind the existing gates (`npm test`, `npm run e2e`, `npx tsx dogfood/parity.ts`, live wire verification against the standing room, findings appended to `STATUS.md`). Before rung 1, two things that are not code: freeze the vocabulary (`home`, one derivation rule, the 0.1.8 field list from section 8), because every artifact in this wave rewrites if it lands second; and send spike 2's seven questions, so the clock starts while the code gets written.

### v0.6.0 (rung 1): a stranger's LangGraph agent joins and completes one task

**Delivers.** `deploy/peers.json` plus `room_admin invite` minting a single-use expiring `invite_token` bound to a `peer_id`; `room_join` accepting it; `home` stamped on the member record, presence, roster and `envelope.from`, defaulting to `"local"` for any principal with no invite; the transport-principal linkage rule (a call is rejected if its membership was admitted under a different principal); membership expiry tied to the admission record's `expires_at`; `history_visibility: "joined_after"` forced for `home !== "local"` plus the `since` clamp to a new persisted `joinedAtSeq`; quarantine and eviction keyed on `peer_id`; `system {event: "admitted"}`; a static audience-bound scoped bearer required in front of the MCP handler and on `room_create`; `capabilities.extensions["io.github.pbeneteau/rooms"] = {version, profiles}` in `server/discover`; SDK validation failures wrapped in the RFA error envelope as `bad_request`; `wrapped` in `room_listen` results; `INTEROP.md` with the six-call minimum, the per-framework timeout table (era-stamped) and `rfa_min.py` **including the boundary wrapper and name allowlist**; an `Authorization` header path in `src/client.ts` `rawCall` (which today sends none), which is also the first half of the reverse topology.

**Proves it works on the live wire.** Spike 1: the cold-start container joins with an invite, claims a task, completes it with evidence, and leaves, driven only by `INTEROP.md`. `npm run remote-conformance` walks join, claim, complete-with-evidence and leave. A replayed invite fails with `invite_invalid`; a join with the right invite but a mismatched key fails with `join_denied`; a rename after quarantine fails; an unauthenticated `POST /mcp` and an uncredentialed `room_create` both fail. Spike 5's `room_listen{since: 0}` returns zero events for the guest. All 85 tests, both e2e suites and the parity gate stay green.

**Excludes.** REST binding. OAuth proper and RFC 9728 metadata. Signatures, receipts, anchoring. Scopes. Probation. Per-zone card projection. Delegation chains. Webhooks. Anything for a peer that has not been named.

### v0.6.1 (rung 2): the claim is a lease, so a crashed peer does not wedge the board

**Delivers.** `attempt` and `lease_expires` on the task; `claim_token` returned in the claim result only and never logged; `complete`/`update`/`release` accepting it; principal-bound re-binding on a new member id; release on the existing offline transition, on `leave` and on eviction, emitting `task_released`; `max_attempts` default 1 with opt-in requeue; a one-lease-period restart grace; `max_claims_per_member` default 3; a separate task-action rate counter keyed on `peer_id` (so leave-and-rejoin does not reset it); a per-peer membership cap; `lease_expired` finally thrown.

**Proves it works on the live wire.** Spike 7 end to end. A guest that claims and is `kill -9`ed releases its task and a second member picks it up. The killed guest rejoins with a fresh `m_` id, presents its `claim_token`, and completes the work it actually did. A stale token gets `lease_expired` with recoverable data. A guest that claims four tasks is refused the fourth.

**Excludes.** `lease_ttl_s` as a policy with a clamp, `task_lease_grace_s`, progress as a new verb (fold lease renewal and a cadence limit into the existing `update`), `claim_policy` enums, structured multi-part results, artifact digests and any hub-side URL dereferencing.

### v0.6.2 (rung 3): the gate and the human see what the peer sent

**Delivers.** `evaluateGate` over mutating `room_task` actions (title, description, note, evidence summary and text parts) with a versioned check-input contract and a defined `hold`-to-`refuse` degradation; size caps on every task field; the sanitizer character set (C0, bidi, zero-width, TAG block, whitespace folding) applied in `wrapForModel` and the task-text wrapper; `evidence_required` forced at **claim** time when the claimer is not `home: "local"`; the verifier required to be local, the creator, or human-origin, with `verifier_home` recorded and rejections capped; the approval ext requiring structured `{tool_name, input_preview}` with hub-stamped requester provenance including `home`, sanitized and elision-marked; the console showing `home` and a short member id beside every name, a room-level "this room has guests" header, and the coarse provenance list (non-local members whose content entered the run's context, from the checkpoint `room_cursor`); `gate.json` gaining one guest rule that survived spike 9.

**Proves it works on the live wire.** Spike 6: a TAG-block payload in a task note is stripped from the model-facing form and visible in the log. Spike 9's replay count is acceptable. A guest completes a task, the card shows requester `home` and a sanitized preview, and a second guest membership from the same peer cannot verify it. A guest that rejects three completions in a row is rate-limited.

**Excludes.** Per-skill visibility. Probation as a member state. Egress byte budgets. A behavioural watchdog. Model-backed egress screening (that is a knob nobody turns on until spike 9's false-refuse rate is known).

### v0.6.3 (rung 4): the audit artifact becomes real, and the room store stops losing rooms

**Delivers.** Atomic `writeMeta` (temp file, fsync, rename, fsync directory) with **rebuild-from-log** rather than refuse-to-boot; `writeMeta` before `emit`'s append (or a load path that reconciles tasks from the log) so a winning claim cannot revert; restart-durable send idempotency via `sentIds` with the degraded-result semantics written into spec 9.1; `O_EXCL` plus a heartbeat in `.hub.lock`; `room_admin redact` with the `redacted` system event, blanking the body in the snapshot and all future replay while leaving the chain intact; a retention window stated in the join contract and a per-member event export for a leaving peer; `npm run verify-log <ndjson>` recomputing the chain from genesis and reporting the first divergence; `/healthz` returning `{"ok":true}` publicly with detail behind the session token; `Dockerfile` plus `compose.yaml`; `db.backup()` replacing the `sqlite3` CLI shell-out; `SECURITY.md`, a supported-version line, and CI running `npm test` and `npm run e2e`.

**Proves it works on the live wire.** Spikes 3, 4 and 10. `npm run verify-log` accepts the live log and rejects it after one byte is edited. A redaction blanks a body in the console and in a fresh replay while `verify-log` still passes. `docker compose up` yields a healthy hub and a supervisor that waits for it.

**Excludes.** Chain anchoring and hub receipts. Log windowing (897 KB over 891 events measured; persist `seq` and `chainHead` in the snapshot if boot cost ever matters). Postgres. `node:sqlite`. A single binary.

### v0.6.4 (rung 5): the peer cannot spend your money or hide who approved

**Delivers.** `usd_per_day` per peer, checked by the resident at pickup against the `data/obs.db` ledger v0.5.2 built, with the `overloaded` refusal naming the peer; `member_rpm` scoped per `(sender, recipient home)`; per-human `principal_id` records with `timingSafeEqual` comparison, stamped on the membership, on every intervention and on every approval decision; per-principal console memberships with exact-name matching; the retrieval set and gate verdict recorded on every answer run (forensics only, no detector claim); `source_origin` weighting in `memoryfs.retrieve()` with decay for agent-origin facts, gated on the parity fixtures.

**Proves it works on the live wire.** A guest driving questions past its daily ceiling gets `overloaded` with a nonzero cost row. Two human principals approve two cards and the log names each. Spike 8 runs and its verdict is recorded either way.

**Excludes.** A grant matrix. Cross-tenant human principals (a partner's PM cannot be a human principal without receiving a key, which is the wrong shape; that needs per-principal provisioning and is its own decision). Any second scheduled job.

### v0.6.5 (rung 6): the decision, not a feature

**Delivers.** Sixty days after rung 1's cold-start guest passes: either a named peer exists and the park list's triggers start firing in the order that peer's answers dictate, or the answer was **deepen local** and the remaining thirty-odd recommendations stay parked with their triggers intact. In the second case the deliverable is the same as wave 03's: more residents, more workloads, more eval cases, and the cold-start guest kept green as a standing regression.

**Proves it works.** A written answer in `STATUS.md` with the peer's name or the sentence "no peer named, parked".

**Excludes.** Building OAuth, REST, signatures, receipts, delegation chains or federation for an organization that has not been named.
